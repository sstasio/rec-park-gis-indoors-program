GIS Field Equipment & Services Procurement Brief

With decision justification — Rec & Park Infrastructure Assessment Program

Executive Summary

This brief recommends a minimal, service-first equipment approach for building the department's GIS capture program across building interiors, outdoor hardscape, and underground utilities. The guiding principle throughout: own only what is used continuously and cheaply maintained; rent or contract anything specialized, infrequent, or already available through an existing city resource. Total new hardware spend is under $2,500, with aerial imagery needs met through existing/low-cost service accounts (EagleView, Spexi) rather than a drone purchase.

Justification — Why Each Decision Was Made

1. RTK GNSS Receiver (Bad Elf Flex Mini) — BUY

Every capture stream in this program depends on accurate positioning tied to the department's California State Plane (WKID 7132) coordinate standard. Uncorrected phone GPS is accurate to only a few meters; an RTK receiver brings that down to centimeter level.

2. Data Governance & Schema Standards — FORMALIZE (no cost)

An undocumented or inconsistent ID/attribute scheme becomes far more expensive to fix retroactively than to define up front.

3. EagleView (existing city account) — USE AS PRIMARY OUTDOOR IMAGERY SOURCE

The department already has access to this account at the city level, at no incremental cost, with true oblique imagery and AI-driven measurement tools.

4. Spexi — KEEP, BUT DEMOTE TO SUPPLEMENTAL/ON-DEMAND ONLY

Spexi's on-demand model fills the gap of needing fresh imagery outside EagleView's scheduled capture cycle.

5. In-House Mapping Drone — DO NOT BUY

Rejected due to Part 107 compliance overhead, FCC import restrictions pushing cost to $7K–15K, and redundancy with EagleView/Spexi.

6. Rugged Phone Cases & Battery Packs — BUY

Staff phones are now primary field survey instruments; cheap insurance against field damage and downtime.

7. GPR / Utility Locating — CONTRACT PER-PROJECT

Specialized, infrequent (Phase 3 only) — contracting avoids owning equipment and training staff for occasional use.

Guiding principle applied throughout: buy only what is used constantly and is cheap to maintain; use what the city already provides before paying for anything new; keep low-cost/no-commitment services for gap-filling needs; contract out anything specialized or infrequent.
